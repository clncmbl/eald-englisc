The version of "The Battle of Brunanburh" discussed on this site comes from the Parker Chronicle, which is the oldest of the surviving manuscripts of the Anglo-Saxon Chronicles. Parker Chronicle is also referred to as the Winchester Chronicle and also as Manuscript A or Version A or Recension A of the Anglo-Saxon Chronicles. The poem is also found in the the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle manuscripts B, C, and D with minor variations. Like Manuscript A, these manuscripts are referred to by other names as well.

The name Winchester Chronicle reflects the general consensus that both it and the original Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, from which it was, directly or indirectly, copied, were written in Winchester in the early 890s. Winchester was the seat of court of King Alfred the Great, who initially ordered the compilation of an Anglo-Saxon chronicle. According to Parkes, the manuscript moved from Winchester to Canterbury in about 1005. In Canterbury, it continued to have entries added in Old English with the last being for the year 1070.

Eventually, possession of the manuscript passed to Archbishop of Canterbury Matthew Parker, who died in 1575. According to the Parker Library at Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, Parker donated the manuscript to Corpus Christi College, Cambridge along with many other manuscripts in 1574. His association with the manuscript is what led it to be called the Parker Chronicle.

The manuscript is currently located in the Parker Library at Corpus Christi College and is bound in a codex with the listing title as "Cambridge, Corpus Christi College, MS 173: The Parker Chronicle". In addition to the Parker Chronicle, the codex includes other works such as the Old English laws of Alfred the Great and some works by Sedulius. The codex is sometimes referred to by the chronicle name because the chronicle is the most notable work in the codex.
