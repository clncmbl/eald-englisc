The shieldwall cleaved, hēowan ⌜heaþo-linde⌝ _ ⌜hamora lāfan⌝, hewed battle-linden with leavings of hammers, --- ⌜Hēr⌝ This poem is the entry for a year (937) in the Parker manuscript of the Anglo-Saxon Chronicles. As such, it begins with the Old English word for "here" which in this context could be interpreted as "in this year". Given the fact that word causes the line to break metrical rules for Old English poetry, we can reasonably assume that the original version of this poem did not include the word. Apparently, the scribe decided to put an existing poem directly into the manuscript and prefix it with "Her". --- ⌜clufan⌝ The subject of clufan is "afaran" two lines down. "The sons cleaved the shieldwall." --- ⌜heaþolinde⌝ battle-linden; a kenning for shields, which were sometimes made of linden wood. --- ⌜hamora lāfan⌝ leavings of hammers; a kenning for swords alluding to the use of hammers in making them. +++ afaran Ēadweardes, _ swā him ġeæþele wæs sons of Edward, as for them natural was from cnēomǣġum, _ þæt hī æt campe oft from ancestors, that they at combat often wiþ lāþra ġehwæne _ land ealgodon, against each of the enemies land defended, hord ond hāmas. _ Hettend crungun, | 10 hoard and homes. This seems most appropriate among the various other fixes offered. The infinitive is "dynian" (or "dynnan"), which is cognate with PDE "din". There are two similar uses of dynede in Beowulf. On line 766, Beowulf has the short sentence, "Dryhtsele dynede." (The noble hall resounded.) On line 2559 is, "Hruse dynede." (The ground resounded.) +++ seċġas hwāte, _ siðþan sunne up bold warriors, from when the sun [went] up on morgentīd, _ mǣre ⌜tungol⌝, in the morning, glorious heavenly body, --- ⌜tungol⌝ Although tungol is usually translated as "star" here, the word is not limited to stars and the Anglo-Saxons would not have known that the sun is a star. +++ glād ofer grundas, _ godes condel beorht, | 15 glided over plains, God's bright candle, ēċes drihtnes, _ oð sīo æþele ġesċeaft eternal Lord's, until the noble creation sāh tō setle. _ Þǣr ⌜læġ seċġ mæniġ⌝ sank to resting place.
